\begin{abstract}
Whether stratospheric variability modulates threshold-governed natural hazards is unknown. Here we show that natural dry-slab avalanche counts in the Swiss Alps decrease by two-thirds during stratospheric sudden warming (SSW) windows (14/16 events suppressed; rate ratio $= 0.32$; sign test $P = 0.002$). Concurrently, human-triggered fatal-incident rates increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$), revealing a loaded-gun state in which weather-regime redistribution loads structural hazard while suppressing natural triggers. Regional Alpine Z500 mediates the surface response ($r = 0.56$, $P = 0.024$); SSW retains a significant daily-level association after Z500 adjustment under negative-binomial regression ($\mathrm{IRR} = 0.55$, $P = 0.005$) but not under winter-clustered GEE ($P = 0.20$), bracketing the true event-level signal between these estimates. SSW carries no standalone forecast skill (leave-one-out Brier skill score $= -0.95$). Suppression is temporally symmetric around onset (pre-onset deficit 52\%, post-onset 48\%; $P_{\mathrm{diff}} = 0.98$), consistent with planetary wave forcing as a common driver of both the SSW and the surface hazard response, though indistinguishable from simultaneous downward propagation at this sample size. Directionally consistent suppression appears across five countries sharing the same SSW events (multi-system corroboration, not independent replication). This loaded-gun mechanism---opposing-direction loading and trigger suppression under a single circulation forcing---extends the preconditioned-event category of compound-hazard typology to systems where a single forcing drives loading and triggering in opposite directions.
\end{abstract}

Stratospheric sudden warming events are among the strongest disturbances of the winter polar vortex and are followed by persistent surface anomalies including cold outbreaks, blocked circulation and precipitation shifts\cite{Matsuno1971,Baldwin2001,Kidston2015}. Those responses are well documented for temperature and precipitation extremes---the February 2021 Texas cold-air outbreak, linked to a polar-vortex disruption, caused over 200 deaths and \$100~billion in damages\cite{Cohen2021}---but whether SSWs can substantially modulate threshold-governed geophysical hazards beyond meteorology remains unclear. Avalanches provide a stringent test because release depends on multiple meteorological trigger conditions acting on a preconditioned snowpack, and because independent occurrence, forecast and process-model records are available\cite{Schweizer2003,Reuter2018}.

Any avalanche anomaly near SSW onset has at least two physically plausible interpretations. SSW events are preceded by enhanced planetary wave activity\cite{Polvani2004} that simultaneously reorganises tropospheric circulation; the avalanche anomaly could therefore reflect this shared tropospheric reorganisation (common cause) rather than a top-down stratospheric influence. Alternatively, post-onset downward coupling could contribute an additional component. Distinguishing these interpretations requires event timing, cross-region consistency and mechanism tests.

Here we test whether SSW windows reorganise avalanche trigger environments strongly enough to alter natural dry slab occurrence across 16 events over 21 Swiss winters. We then ask whether the anomaly is better explained by wholesale snowpack stabilisation or by selective trigger suppression---a dissociation we term the ``loaded-gun'' mechanism because it simultaneously increases structural hazard while decreasing observed events.

\section*{Results}

\noindent\textit{Study origin.} This study evolved from an exploratory geomagnetic hypothesis to the SSW framework reported here; the effective multiple-testing burden is difficult to quantify. The primary confirmatory test is the Swiss sign test (14/16 events); all supporting analyses are labelled exploratory. Hypothesis tests are corrected with BH-FDR stratified into event-level, daily-level and external families (Supplementary Tables~7 and \ref{tab:fdr_stratified}).

\subsection*{Natural dry slab counts decline during SSW windows}

We analysed daily natural-trigger dry slab avalanche counts from the Swiss WSL/SLF database across 21 winters (1998/99--2018/19) for 16 SSW events\cite{Butler2015}. Fourteen of 16 events yield $\mathrm{RR} < 1$ (sign test $P = 0.002$; Fig.~\ref{fig:ssw_matched}). The geometric mean rate ratio is $\mathrm{RR} = 0.32$ (95\% CI $[0.26, 0.53]$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:winter_boot}), corresponding to a 68\% reduction ($d = -1.06$). All leave-one-out folds remain significant (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:loo}), and a 180-specification curve returns $\mathrm{RR} < 1$ for every variant.

Suppression is already present before the wind reversal: 13/16 events show lower activity in the pre-onset interval ($-15$ to $-6$~d; median $\mathrm{RR} = 0.16$, $P = 0.011$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:phases}). A temporal decomposition shows that 52\% of the total avalanche deficit occurs before formal SSW onset, but the pre-onset and post-onset deficits are statistically indistinguishable (177 vs.\ 164 counts; paired Wilcoxon $P = 0.98$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:pre_post_decomp}). This temporal symmetry is consistent with a common-cause interpretation in which planetary-wave forcing reorganises both stratosphere and troposphere simultaneously\cite{Polvani2004}, but it is equally consistent with simultaneous downward propagation or a combination of both pathways; the $n = 16$ sample cannot distinguish these. Swiss and Norwegian lag profiles peak at $-13$ and $-12$~days, respectively (Spearman $r = 0.62$, $P = 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$).

\subsection*{Multi-country replication and boundary conditions}

All four Norwegian SSW events show lower NVE danger than controls ($\Delta = -0.48$), all four Utah events show reduced dry slab activity ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.34$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:utah}), and three of four French BRA events are negative ($\Delta = -0.15$; Supplementary Table~26b--c). A cross-country synthesis harmonises evidence from five countries using a binary suppression endpoint (event-level decrease vs.\ non-decrease): 27 of 30 country-events show suppression (binomial $P < 10^{-5}$), with pooled log-odds ratio $= 1.35$ ($P = 0.003$, $I^2 = 0\%$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:meta_forest}). Because all countries respond to the \emph{same} SSW events, these 30 country-events are not statistically independent; the synthesis demonstrates multi-system corroboration across independent measurement platforms (occurrence counts, danger forecasts, process-model output) rather than independent replication sensu stricto. The meta-analytic confidence interval may understate uncertainty owing to this shared-event structure.

The Canadian archive shows the opposite sign nationally ($\Delta = +0.37$), but owner-stratified analysis resolves this into a maritime west-Canadian increase and a continental Qu\'ebec decrease ($\Delta = -0.94$; Supplementary Table~26d--e). This internal split follows the direction predicted by avalanche-climate theory\cite{McClung2006}: continental snow climates respond like the Alps, while maritime climates may receive enhanced loading. Sensitivity analysis without western Canada strengthens the pooled effect (log-OR $= 1.62$, $P = 0.0006$).

\subsection*{Loaded-gun mechanism: natural down, human up}

The differential trigger response is the central mechanistic finding. Natural dry slab counts decrease strongly ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.32$) while danger ratings \emph{increase} in 11/14 events and human-triggered counts across all types increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.45$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:human}). Austrian LAWIS incident data, dominated by human-triggered encounters, show the same sign. The resulting count--rating dissociation rules out a simple ``SSW stabilises the snowpack'' interpretation: SSW windows preserve or intensify loaded snowpack states without restoring the spontaneous-release pathways required for a natural dry-slab cycle.

In the independent 55-year accident record ($n = 29$ events, 4,868 records), fatal-incident rates increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$, 95\% CI $[1.00, 1.95]$; event-level $P = 0.067$). A Cochran--Mantel--Haenszel test stratified by decade yields $\mathrm{OR} = 1.77$ (design-effect-corrected $P = 0.007$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:gee_accident}), demonstrating that the secular recreation trend does not confound the signal. The 12 pre-1998 events show the same direction ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.43$, 95\% CI $[0.78, 2.64]$). The January 2021 SSW---post-dating all calibration data by two years---produced the highest post-2019 fatality count ($\mathrm{RR} = 2.52$ in the 30-day post-onset window; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:prospective_2021}).

\subsection*{Circulation mediates the surface response}

Regional Alpine Z500 explains 31\% of event-level variance ($r = 0.56$, $P = 0.024$; bootstrap CI $[0.22, 0.78]$; Spearman $\rho = 0.59$, $P = 0.015$; Fig.~\ref{fig:era5_composites}), substantially outperforming hemispheric AO/NAO ($R^2 < 0.01$), consistent with regional mediation of SSW surface impacts\cite{Kretschmer2018}. Influence diagnostics indicate no single event dominates: leave-one-out yields $r = 0.50$--$0.62$ across all 16 folds (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:z500_diag}).

After controlling for Z500, stratospheric vortex deceleration adds no residual signal ($\rho_{\mathrm{partial}} = -0.28$, $P = 0.29$), whereas Z500 retains predictive power after controlling for the stratosphere ($\rho_{\mathrm{partial}} = 0.64$, $P = 0.008$). Composite stratospheric diagnostics confirm the classic downward-propagation signature (temperature anomalies peak at $+17.6$~K at 10~hPa and descend to $+6.1$~K at 100~hPa by lag $+20$~days; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:downward_prop}). EP-flux diagnostics quantify the wave-driven deceleration: composite $du/dt$ at 10~hPa reaches $-2.12$~m~s$^{-1}$~d$^{-1}$ at onset ($P < 10^{-4}$), consistent with strong EP-flux convergence; the deceleration propagates from 10 to 100~hPa in $\sim$6.6~days (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:epflux_diag}). Direct composite $\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa confirms that wave forcing is systematically elevated before every SSW event: the pre-onset anomaly averages $+9.4$~K$\cdot$m~s$^{-1}$ ($t = 7.7$, $P < 10^{-5}$; peaking at $+17.5$~K$\cdot$m~s$^{-1}$ at lag~$-1$~d; 2.1$\times$ DJF climatology; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:vt_composite}). The \emph{event-level} dose--response is null ($\rho = -0.25$, $P = 0.34$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:direct_vt}), but this reflects range restriction: all SSW events require strong wave forcing by definition, so cross-event variability in the stratospheric ``dose'' is compressed relative to the tropospheric dose (Z500), which varies with the regional background state. SSW onset precedes peak Alpine blocking by $25 \pm 13$~days (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:blocking_lead}), providing a potential forecast lead-time window.

\subsection*{SSW retains an independent association beyond tropospheric blocking}

To test whether SSW marks additional regime information beyond what Z500 alone captures, we fitted daily count regression models to natural dry slab counts using the full 21-winter panel ($n = 2{,}702$ days). Severe overdispersion ($\hat{\phi} = 44$; variance-to-mean ratio) and excess zeros (60\% of days) necessitate models that account for both features. We compared six specifications spanning the Poisson, negative-binomial (NB) and zero-inflated families, with GEE as the most conservative clustering-corrected estimator (Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:incremental}; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:nb_comparison}).

Under negative-binomial regression (NB2)---the specification that correctly handles overdispersion without requiring zero-inflation---SSW retains significance after Z500 adjustment ($\mathrm{IRR} = 0.55$, $P = 0.005$). The zero-inflated negative-binomial (ZINB), which has the lowest AIC/BIC (Vuong $Z = -4.2$, $P < 0.0001$ vs.\ ZIP), also retains SSW significance ($\mathrm{IRR} = 0.40$, $P < 0.0001$); however, Z500 loses significance in the ZINB count component ($\mathrm{IRR} = 1.03$, $P = 0.84$), indicating collinearity between the zero-inflation and count sub-models that redistributes explanatory weight across components. Under GEE with winter-level exchangeable correlation---the most conservative specification, directly accounting for the 21-winter clustering structure---the SSW association weakens to $\mathrm{IRR} = 0.71$ ($P = 0.20$). This attenuation reflects the fundamental $n = 16$ event-level constraint: with only 16 SSW events distributed across 21 winters, winter-level clustering absorbs most effective degrees of freedom. The true event-level signal is therefore bracketed between the NB2 estimate ($P = 0.005$, treating days as independent conditional on covariates) and the GEE estimate ($P = 0.20$, treating winters as the effective sampling unit). Z500 is uniformly significant across all non-zero-inflated specifications ($P < 0.002$), confirming its role as the proximate mediator. The SSW binary flag marks additional regime structure in models that treat days as independent or account for overdispersion, but its independence from Z500 does not survive the most conservative clustering correction and should be regarded as suggestive rather than confirmatory.

\subsection*{Weather--hazard amplification}

The composite weather shift is $d_{\mathrm{weather}} = 0.69$ but the hazard response is $d_{\mathrm{hazard}} = 1.06$---consistent with simultaneous weakening of multiple trigger channels. Independent-channel solutions with $k = 6$--$8$ and correlated-channel solutions with $\sim$1--3 effective channels all reproduce the observed RR, whereas $k = 1$ does not (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:threshold_amp}, \ref{tab:threshold_loo}, \ref{tab:corr_sensitivity}). The inference is structural: multi-channel triggering amplifies modest weather anomalies into a large hazard response.

\subsection*{Robustness}

The signal survives leave-one-out, specification-curve (180 variants, 100\% directional consistency), Bayesian ($\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$), temporal-heterogeneity and alternative-catalog analyses (Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:loo}, \ref{tab:sensitivity}; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:spec_curve}). The 180 specification-curve variants share the same underlying data and differ only in analytical choices (window length, count metric, outlier treatment); their consistency demonstrates robustness to researcher degrees of freedom but not independent replication, because neighbouring variants are highly correlated ($r > 0.9$ for variants differing by one parameter). Three falsification tests confirm signal specificity: (1)~placebo dates shifted $\pm$60~days yield no suppression (gmRR $= 0.90$ and $0.68$, respectively); (2)~weak-vortex non-SSW winters (matched by U10 deceleration) show no effect (gmRR $= 1.06$, $P = 0.80$); (3)~random-date permutations produce the observed 14/16 sign consistency in only 7.3\% of iterations (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:placebo}). Leave-two-out analysis across all 120 event-pair combinations confirms that no pair of events drives the result: all folds maintain gmRR $< 1$ (range 0.35--0.55; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:l2o}).

The post-2005 subperiod ($n = 9$) preserves the direction ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.45$) but loses significance ($P = 0.18$). Power simulation indicates that $n = 9$ events at the full-period effect size yield only 37.9\% power to detect significance at $\alpha = 0.05$; the observed $P = 0.18$ falls at the 69th percentile of simulated outcomes, so the post-2005 subset is underpowered to distinguish persistence from attenuation (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:power_sim}). Natural dry slabs are specifically suppressed: wet natural avalanches show the opposite direction ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.62$, $P = 0.038$), observer effort is not the driver (total reports \emph{increase} during SSW windows), and human-triggered counts across all types also increase.

\subsection*{Snowpack mechanism: loading without triggering}

SNOWPACK diagnostics across 130 Swiss stations and 292,837 station-days characterise the loaded-gun state. Antecedent snowpack conditions in the 30-to-16-day pre-onset window are indistinguishable between SSW and non-SSW years: snow depth ($P = 0.32$), SWE ($P = 0.32$) and persistent-weak-layer prevalence ($P = 0.93$) show no difference (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:pre_event_snowpack}). The trigger-suppression effect therefore operates entirely within the SSW meteorological forcing window.

Within that window, SSW conditions show increased loading (HN24 $+9.8\%$, SWE $+8.1\%$), greater persistent-weak-layer prevalence and lower modelled stability indices---conditions that should favour natural release, not suppress it. The count decline cannot reflect wholesale snowpack stabilisation. Instead, the proximate triggers are selectively suppressed: snow-surface warming events $-57\%$, rain-on-snow $-29\%$, incoming shortwave radiation $-17.5\%$. Precipitation-phase analysis explains the loading paradox: the snow fraction increases by 12~pp while rainfall decreases by 34\% ($P < 10^{-3}$). Cold, low-density snowfall loads the snowpack structurally without providing thermal or hydraulic perturbations for spontaneous release (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:precip_phase}).

A four-regime weather decomposition shows that SSW windows nearly double the frequency of cold-dry days while halving warm-wet days. Within regimes, SSW and non-SSW days have near-identical avalanche rates in three of four cases, indicating that the association operates primarily through regime redistribution rather than within-regime rate changes. When weather regime and day-of-year are controlled simultaneously, the residual SSW effect is $\mathrm{IRR} = 0.89$ ($P = 0.06$), consistent with regime redistribution as the primary pathway.

Independent Rutschblock tests show a small but significant stability increase ($d = 0.10$, $P = 0.003$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:rutsch}). This does not contradict the loaded-gun mechanism. The Rutschblock tests point-scale propagation propensity under standardised loading\cite{Schweizer2008}; the stability increase reflects suppressed trigger propagation under cold-dry conditions, not the absence of weak layers. Field campaigns confirm that persistent weak layers (facets, depth hoar) form preferentially under precisely these conditions\cite{Reuter2018}. The loaded-gun mechanism operates at \emph{landscape} scale: structural instability persists within the snowpack, but the triggers that would release it (warming, rain-on-snow) are suppressed, so that point-scale stability tests register improved scores even as the snowpack retains buried weaknesses. The SNOWPACK grain-type analysis (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:snowpack_microstructure}) confirms elevated faceted-layer fractions during SSW windows ($+18\%$; $P = 0.012$), consistent with the Rutschblock results reflecting surface rather than basal conditions.

\section*{Discussion}

We report that Swiss natural dry slab avalanche counts fall by about two-thirds during SSW windows---the first event-level evidence that stratospheric variability is associated with a discrete natural-hazard response. The signal is directionally consistent across four countries and two independent model chains, with structured regional dependence in Canada. 

The partial-correlation analysis shows that Z500 is the proximate mediator ($\rho = 0.64$, $P = 0.008$), and the SSW label's added predictive value beyond Z500 is not significant at $n = 16$ ($\rho = -0.28$, $P = 0.29$). This does not make the SSW connection redundant---it reframes it. The scientific contribution is not ``SSW causes avalanche suppression'' but rather: (1)~SSW events identify a specific subset of blocking episodes that produce the loaded-gun mechanism (non-SSW blocking suppresses 82\% of the time via fair-weather stagnation; SSW-associated blocking suppresses only 42\%; $P = 0.013$); (2)~the loaded-gun mechanism itself---simultaneous loading and trigger suppression---is a previously uncharacterised compound-event sub-type; and (3)~SSW events are forecast with 1--2~week lead time\cite{Sigmond2013}, providing operational utility as a recognisable marker of these coupled episodes even though the surface expression depends on tropospheric state.

\paragraph{Compound-event sub-type.} SSW-driven cold-air outbreaks have been linked to energy-grid failures\cite{Cohen2021}, excess mortality\cite{Gasparrini2015} and transport disruption, but these are continuous-response systems. Avalanches are a threshold-governed system where release requires specific trigger conditions. Within the typology of Zscheischler et al.\cite{Zscheischler2020}, the loaded-gun mechanism extends the preconditioned-event category: rather than sequential forcings (e.g., drought then heatwave), a single contemporaneous circulation forcing drives loading and trigger suppression in \emph{opposing directions}. SSW-associated weather-regime redistribution loads structural hazard (snow depth SWE $+26\%$, persistent weak layers elevated) while suppressing natural triggers (warming events $-57\%$, rain-on-snow $-29\%$), so that fewer natural releases occur even as structural instability increases. The trigger-budget quantification clarifies this dissociation: warming-event decrease ($-22\%$, $P < 0.001$) and radiation decrease ($-17.5\%$) dominate the wind-loading increase ($\sim$12\% of trigger weight; net weighted $\Delta = +2.9\%$; 95\% CI $[-10, +17]$\%; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:trigger_budget}). The net trigger budget therefore spans zero, consistent with the four-regime analysis showing that the suppression operates primarily through regime redistribution (shifting the frequency of cold-dry days) rather than through within-regime rate changes. The human-triggered accident increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$ over 29 events spanning 55 years; design-effect-corrected CMH $P = 0.007$; event-level $P = 0.067$) indicates that risk to recreationists \emph{increases} during SSW windows even as natural releases decline. The 2021 prospective winter produced the highest post-2019 fatality count, consistent with this prediction. The French BRA suppression---using the S2M/SAFRAN/Crocus chain\cite{Vernay2022}, entirely independent of ERA5/SNOWPACK---provides cross-model validation.

\paragraph{SSW transforms blocking character.} Non-SSW blocking episodes suppress natural activity in 82\% of cases (fair-weather stagnation), whereas SSW-associated blocking suppresses in only 42\% (Mann--Whitney $P = 0.013$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:blocking_comparison}). SSW identifies \emph{which} blocking episodes produce the loaded-gun state: those where active wave forcing sustains moisture transport alongside cold advection, loading the snowpack rather than stabilising it.

\paragraph{Climate change context.} SSW events occur in $\sim$60\% of Northern Hemisphere winters\cite{Charlton2007}, a frequency projected to remain stable through 2100\cite{Ayarzaguena2020}. The loaded-gun mechanism is therefore not transient. At the observed accident RR of 1.40, the upper-bound population-attributable fraction is $\sim$19\%---approximately 5 excess fatalities per SSW winter at current Swiss rates.

\paragraph{Coupled wave-driven mechanism.} The timing evidence constrains but does not resolve the causal pathway. Suppression begins before the stratospheric wind reversal: 13/16 events show lower activity in the pre-onset interval ($-15$ to $-6$~d; median $\mathrm{RR} = 0.16$, $P = 0.011$), coinciding with the enhanced upward wave-activity flux that precedes SSW onset by 7--10~days\cite{Polvani2004}. However, the temporal distribution of the deficit is symmetric around onset (52\% pre-onset, 48\% post-onset; $P_{\mathrm{diff}} = 0.98$), which is consistent with three interpretations: (a)~common-cause planetary-wave forcing reorganises tropospheric circulation simultaneously with the SSW; (b)~downward propagation reinforces the surface response after onset; or (c)~both operate concurrently. At $n = 16$ events, these cannot be distinguished. Three additional lines of evidence constrain the mechanism without resolving it: (i)~regional Z500 shows a dose--response ($r = 0.56$, $P = 0.024$), whereas direct $\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa does not ($\rho = -0.25$, $P = 0.34$); (ii)~Granger causality analysis shows bidirectional coupling (stratosphere-to-troposphere $F = 45.6$; troposphere-to-stratosphere $F = 26.9$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:granger}); (iii)~composite Z500 reaches its nadir at $\sim$$+10.5$~days after SSW onset (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lead_time}), indicating a secondary post-onset deepening consistent with downward-propagation reinforcement. The SSW event is therefore best understood as a \emph{co-symptom} of the wave forcing rather than a unidirectional cause, with potential downward-propagation reinforcement. The SSW label remains operationally useful as a recognisable marker of these coupled wave-driven episodes\cite{Sigmond2013} and warrants prospective evaluation as one element in multi-predictor situational-awareness frameworks, although it carries no standalone forecast skill (LOO BSS $= -0.95$).

\paragraph{Limitations.} $n = 16$ events constrain all mediation and causal analyses; the post-2005 subperiod ($n = 9$) preserves direction but loses significance ($P = 0.18$; power $= 38\%$). The extended accident analysis ($n = 29$) yields event-level $P = 0.067$; the design-effect-corrected CMH result ($P = 0.007$) uses a different statistical model. Only Switzerland and Utah provide count-level replication; Norway and France provide ordinal directional checks. The Canadian reversal follows avalanche-climate-zone predictions but is incompletely characterised. The project's exploratory origin means the effective multiple-testing burden is unquantifiable; only 3/12 event-level tests survive BH-FDR. Single-author pipeline; open code enables but does not substitute for independent replication. The SSW flag carries no standalone forecast skill (LOO BSS $= -0.95$): its surface expression depends on the pre-existing tropospheric state. The event-level dose--response between direct wave forcing ($\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa) and avalanche RR is null ($P = 0.34$), consistent with range restriction within the SSW event class.

These results establish an observational association between stratospheric circulation variability and a threshold-governed natural hazard. The loaded-gun mechanism---in which a single forcing simultaneously increases structural hazard loading while suppressing natural triggers---extends the preconditioned-event category of the Zscheischler et al.\cite{Zscheischler2020} compound-hazard typology. Standard preconditioned events involve sequential forcings (e.g., drought then heatwave); the loaded-gun variant is distinguished by \emph{opposing-direction} effects of a \emph{single contemporaneous} forcing on loading and triggering, creating a delayed-release hazard whose risk increases even as observed event frequency decreases. This mechanism may operate in other threshold-governed geophysical systems where loading and triggering respond to a shared environmental forcing with opposite signs---a hypothesis that warrants quantitative investigation beyond the avalanche domain.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Datasets}

The primary outcome was the Swiss WSL/SLF daily count of natural-trigger dry slab avalanches ($\geq$size~1) across 21 winters (1998/99--2018/19; 7,518~days). An independent outcome---the SLF fatal and near-fatal incident database (1970--2025; 4,868 records)---was used for extended historical ($n = 29$) and prospective (January~2021) analyses. Supporting datasets: Utah Avalanche Center dry slab record (2012--2025), Norwegian NVE danger forecasts (10 inland regions, 2017/18--2022/23), reconstructed M\'et\'eo-France BRA bulletins (14 northern-Alpine massifs, 2016/17--2022/23), Avalanche Canada flexible-forecast panel (post-2022), EAWS danger levels (2011--2015), Swiss EnviDat danger levels and human-triggered counts, ALBINA, Austrian LAWIS incidents (1992--2024), Swiss Rutschblock tests, and SNOWPACK simulations from 130 Swiss stations. Details are in the SI.

\subsection*{SSW identification and matched-control design}

SSW events were identified from the Butler et al.\ catalog\cite{Butler2015} as reversals of the 10~hPa 60$^\circ$N zonal-mean zonal wind. For each event, we computed $\mathrm{RR} = O / E$, where $O$ is the observed count in a $\pm$15-day window and $E$ is the day-of-year-matched expectation from non-SSW winters only ($\pm$3 DOY bandwidth). This design controls seasonality and prevents SSW contamination of the reference climatology. For ordinal danger data we compared SSW and matched-control distributions.

\subsection*{Diagnostics}

ERA5 daily fields for the Swiss Alps (temperature, precipitation, snowfall, wind, radiation) diagnosed surface anomalies. SNOWPACK model output (130 stations, 2004--2013; 11/16 SSW events) provided loading, stability and persistent-weak-layer diagnostics; trigger-frequency metrics were derived from station-level warming, rain-on-snow and radiative conditions. NCEP reanalysis (SLP, Z500, U850) quantified large-scale circulation anomalies across the full 1998--2019 period. Zonal-mean poleward eddy heat flux $\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa (45--75$^\circ$N, cos-latitude-weighted) from NCEP--NCAR reanalysis provided a direct diagnostic of stratospheric wave forcing for each event. The French S2M reanalysis\cite{Vernay2022} (SAFRAN/Crocus/MEPRA chain, independent of ERA5/SNOWPACK) provided cross-model validation; SAFRAN has been validated against 665 independent snow-depth measurements\cite{Durand2009}.

\subsection*{Statistics}

Primary inference used 16 event-level rate ratios assessed with sign test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, $t$-test and permutation test (10,000 random-date sets). Bootstrap CIs from 10,000 resamples; winter-blocked bootstrap confirms robustness (Supplementary Table~34c). BH-FDR correction stratified into event-level, daily-level and external families. Power analysis: exact sign test at $n = 16$ achieves 79\% power at the observed effect; the 180-specification curve achieves 100\% directional consistency; Bayesian $\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$. These benchmarks match the foundational SSW-surface literature ($\sim$18 events in Baldwin \& Dunkerton\cite{Baldwin2001}; 15--25 standard per Domeisen et al.\cite{Domeisen2020}).

\subsection*{Data and code availability}
