\begin{abstract}
Whether a single meteorological forcing can simultaneously increase structural hazard while suppressing observable triggers---an opposing-direction compound event---is unknown. Here we show that stratospheric sudden warming (SSW) windows reduce Swiss natural dry-slab avalanche counts by two-thirds (14/16 events suppressed; geometric mean rate ratio~$= 0.32$, 95\%~CI $[0.26, 0.53]$; sign test $P = 0.004$; event-level sign-randomisation $P_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.01$; Bayes factor $= 17.9$--$178.7$), robust across 180 analytical specifications and three falsification tests. The atmospheric mechanism linking SSW events to European surface cold anomalies and enhanced blocking is independently validated across $\sim$40 events in seven prior studies spanning four reanalysis products; only the final hazard-translation step requires our 16-event sample. SNOWPACK process-model diagnostics across 130 Swiss stations (281,522 station-days) reveal a ``loaded-gun'' mechanism: persistent weak layers shift into the human-triggerable depth band (43\% vs 35\% of station-days within 0.2--1.2~m; critical crack length $-$33\%; $d = 0.31$) while natural triggers are suppressed. Independent validation from the Swiss Avalanche Bulletin confirms the loaded-gun paradox: human experts rate danger `considerable' or higher on 47\% of SSW station-days versus 37\% of controls, yet natural avalanche activity is simultaneously suppressed---a dissociation that can only arise when structural hazard and spontaneous release are decoupled. Human-triggered fatalities increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$; CMH $P = 0.007$). The January 2021 SSW, post-dating all calibration data, directionally confirmed the prediction (15/17 including 2021; $P = 0.001$). SSW events provide 2--4~weeks' lead time beyond surface-regime forecasts; this loaded-gun sub-type extends compound-event theory and identifies a risk-communication blind spot where observed occurrence falls as structural hazard peaks.
\end{abstract}

Stratospheric sudden warming events are among the strongest disturbances of the winter polar vortex and are followed by persistent surface anomalies including cold outbreaks, blocked circulation and precipitation shifts\cite{Matsuno1971,Baldwin2001,Kidston2015}. Those responses are well documented for temperature and precipitation extremes---the February 2021 Texas cold-air outbreak, linked to a polar-vortex disruption, caused over 200 deaths and \$100~billion in damages\cite{Cohen2021}---but whether SSWs can substantially modulate threshold-governed geophysical hazards beyond meteorology remains unclear. Avalanches provide a stringent test because release depends on multiple meteorological trigger conditions acting on a preconditioned snowpack, and because independent occurrence, forecast and process-model records are available\cite{Schweizer2003,Reuter2018}.

Any avalanche anomaly near SSW onset has at least two physically plausible interpretations. SSW events are preceded by enhanced planetary wave activity\cite{Polvani2004} that simultaneously reorganises tropospheric circulation; the avalanche anomaly could therefore reflect this shared tropospheric reorganisation (common cause) rather than a top-down stratospheric influence. Alternatively, post-onset downward coupling could contribute an additional component. Distinguishing these interpretations requires event timing, cross-region consistency and mechanism tests.

Here we test whether SSW windows reorganise avalanche trigger environments strongly enough to alter natural dry slab occurrence across 16 events over 21 Swiss winters. We then ask whether the anomaly is better explained by wholesale snowpack stabilisation or by selective trigger suppression---a dissociation we term the ``loaded-gun'' mechanism because it simultaneously increases structural hazard while decreasing observed events.

\section*{Results}

\noindent\textit{Study origin.} This study evolved from an exploratory geomagnetic hypothesis to the SSW framework reported here; the effective multiple-testing burden is difficult to quantify precisely. The sign test $P = 0.002$ is one-sided, which is appropriate because the physical mechanism (SSW $\to$ cold anomaly $\to$ fewer melt/rain triggers) predicts a specific direction; the two-sided $P = 0.004$ is reported for completeness. A worst-case Bonferroni bound: if $\sim$20 candidate forcings were considered, the one-sided $P_{\mathrm{adjusted}} = 0.002 \times 20 = 0.04$ survives $\alpha = 0.05$. An event-level permutation test that randomly flips the sign of each $\log(\mathrm{RR})$ across 100,000 iterations yields $P = 0.020$ for the observed geometric mean RR; a separate sign-randomisation test using both magnitude and direction gives $P = 0.0005$ (Bonferroni-corrected $P_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.01$; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:randomisation}, \ref{tab:event_robustness}). A Bayesian beta-binomial analysis under a uniform prior yields $P(\theta > 0.5) = 0.975$ (95\% HDI $[0.50, 0.90]$). The primary event-level summary test is the Swiss sign test (14/16 events); all daily-model and dose--response analyses are labelled exploratory. Hypothesis tests are corrected with BH-FDR stratified into event-level, daily-level and external families (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:fdr_stratified}).

\subsection*{Natural dry slab counts decline during SSW windows}

We analysed daily natural-trigger dry slab avalanche counts from the Swiss WSL/SLF database across 21 winters (1998/99--2018/19) for 16 SSW events\cite{Butler2015}. Fourteen of 16 events yield $\mathrm{RR} < 1$ (sign test $P = 0.002$ one-sided, $P = 0.004$ two-sided; Fig.~\ref{fig:ssw_matched}). The geometric mean rate ratio is $\mathrm{RR} = 0.32$ (95\% CI $[0.26, 0.53]$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:winter_boot}), corresponding to a 68\% reduction ($d = -1.06$). The fragility index is 3 at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$: three events would need to change direction to lose significance under the physically directed test. All leave-one-out folds remain significant ($P \leq 0.004$; gmRR range 0.24--0.32; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:loo}), and a 180-specification curve returns $\mathrm{RR} < 1$ for every variant. Lead--lag analysis confirms temporal specificity: 14/16 events show suppression at the SSW-aligned window (lag~0), compared with 7/13 at $-60$~days and 8/12 at $+60$~days (near chance levels). The signal peaks at lag~0 and declines toward the extremes, although a late-lag rebound at $+45$~days (14/16) likely reflects the well-documented 4--6-week persistence of SSW surface impacts\cite{Baldwin2001} (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:lead_lag}).

Suppression is already present before the wind reversal: 13/16 events show lower activity in the pre-onset interval ($-15$ to $-6$~d; median $\mathrm{RR} = 0.16$, $P = 0.011$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:phases}). A temporal decomposition shows that 52\% of the total avalanche deficit occurs before formal SSW onset, but the pre-onset and post-onset deficits are statistically indistinguishable (177 vs.\ 164 counts; paired Wilcoxon $P = 0.98$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:pre_post_decomp}). This temporal symmetry is consistent with a common-cause interpretation in which planetary-wave forcing reorganises both stratosphere and troposphere simultaneously\cite{Polvani2004}, but it is equally consistent with simultaneous downward propagation or a combination of both pathways; the $n = 16$ sample cannot distinguish these. Swiss and Norwegian lag profiles peak at $-13$ and $-12$~days, respectively (Spearman $r = 0.62$, $P = 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$).

\subsection*{Multi-country replication and boundary conditions}

All four Norwegian SSW events show lower NVE danger than controls ($\Delta = -0.48$; $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2$ independent SSW events, as two pairs share windows; Norwegian data for 1998--2012 carry constant fill values, so the effective validation rests on the two post-2013 events only), all four Utah events show reduced dry slab activity ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.34$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:utah}), and French BRA data across all 22 Alpine massifs show a non-significant negative trend ($\Delta = -0.08$, 95\% CI $[-0.19, +0.03]$, $P = 0.083$; Supplementary Table~26b--c). An exploratory N--S latitude gradient within France ($r = -0.81$, $P < 10^{-5}$) concentrates the suppression in the 14 northern massifs ($\Delta = -0.15$), consistent with the latitude dependence of SSW surface impacts\cite{Kretschmer2018}. A cross-country synthesis harmonises evidence from five countries using a binary suppression endpoint (event-level decrease vs.\ non-decrease): 27 of 30 country-events show suppression. Because all countries respond to the \emph{same} SSW events during overlapping periods, the 30 country-events have at most 16 independent event degrees of freedom; the pooled binomial $P < 10^{-5}$ and meta-analytic log-odds ratio $= 1.35$ ($P = 0.003$, $I^2 = 0\%$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:meta_forest}) assume event-level independence and therefore overstate statistical precision. The primary evidential value lies in measurement-system diversity---occurrence counts, professional danger forecasts and process-model output converge on the same direction---not in statistical independence. The Swiss sign test (14/16, $P = 0.002$) is the only properly powered primary inference; multi-country data provide convergent directional corroboration.

The Canadian archive shows the opposite sign nationally ($\Delta = +0.37$), but owner-stratified analysis resolves this into a maritime west-Canadian increase and a continental Qu\'ebec decrease ($\Delta = -0.94$; Supplementary Table~26d--e). This internal split follows the direction predicted by avalanche-climate theory\cite{McClung2006}: continental snow climates respond like the Alps, while maritime climates may receive enhanced loading. Sensitivity analysis without western Canada strengthens the pooled effect (log-OR $= 1.62$, $P = 0.0006$).

\subsection*{Loaded-gun mechanism: natural down, human up}

The differential trigger response is the central mechanistic finding. Natural dry slab counts decrease strongly ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.32$) while danger ratings \emph{increase} in 11/14 events and human-triggered counts across all types increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.45$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:human}). The human-trigger increase is type-specific: wind-slab and mixed-type incidents drive the aggregate elevation, while human-triggered dry slabs are also suppressed ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.68$, $P = 0.126$)---consistent with cold-dry wind-loading creating new wind-slab instabilities rather than uniformly increasing all human-triggered hazard. Austrian LAWIS incident data, dominated by human-triggered encounters, show the same sign.

Independent validation from the Swiss Avalanche Bulletin---a human-expert assessment system not derived from SNOWPACK---confirms the loaded-gun paradox quantitatively: bulletin danger is rated `considerable' (level~$\geq 3$) on 47\% of SSW station-days versus 37\% of controls (OR~$= 1.49$; $n = 38{,}742$ SSW vs $246{,}415$ control station-days; $P < 10^{-10}$). Month-controlled comparisons confirm this is not seasonal confounding (within-month danger elevation in 3/4 winter months; all $P < 10^{-4}$). At the event level, 10/16 events show elevated bulletin danger during the SSW window. The count--rating dissociation rules out a simple ``SSW stabilises the snowpack'' interpretation: SSW windows preserve or intensify loaded snowpack states without restoring the spontaneous-release pathways required for a natural dry-slab cycle.

In the independent 55-year accident record ($n = 29$ events, 4,868 records), fatal-incident rates increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$, 95\% CI $[1.00, 1.95]$; event-level $P = 0.067$). Of the 29 events, 15 show $\mathrm{RR} > 1$ (sign test $P = 0.50$), indicating that the directional consistency is weaker than for natural-slab suppression; the geometric mean $\mathrm{RR} > 1$ is driven by large-magnitude events rather than consistent direction. A Cochran--Mantel--Haenszel test stratified by decade yields $\mathrm{OR} = 1.77$ (design-effect-corrected $P = 0.007$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:gee_accident}), demonstrating that the secular recreation trend does not confound the signal. The 12 pre-1998 events show the same direction ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.43$, 95\% CI $[0.78, 2.64]$). The January 2021 SSW---post-dating all calibration data by two years---produced the highest post-2019 fatality count ($\mathrm{RR} = 2.52$ in the 30-day post-onset window; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:prospective_2021}).

\subsection*{Circulation carries the surface signal}

Regional Alpine Z500 explains 31\% of event-level variance ($r = 0.56$, $P = 0.024$; Fig.~\ref{fig:era5_composites}), substantially outperforming hemispheric AO/NAO ($R^2 < 0.01$), as expected given that SSW surface impacts are regionally heterogeneous\cite{Kretschmer2018}. After controlling for Z500, stratospheric vortex deceleration adds no residual signal ($\rho_{\mathrm{partial}} = -0.28$, $P = 0.29$), whereas Z500 retains predictive power after controlling for the stratosphere ($\rho_{\mathrm{partial}} = 0.64$, $P = 0.008$, survives BH-FDR). This is the expected result under a common-cause pathway: planetary wave forcing simultaneously triggers both the SSW and tropospheric blocking; conditioning on the proximate carrier absorbs the upstream signal. In daily regression, the NB2 model---which handles overdispersion parametrically---supports the SSW predictor ($\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -14.2$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:incremental}); the Poisson $\Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -114$ is inflated by overdispersion ($\hat{\varphi} = 45.1$; quasi-Poisson $F$-test $P = 0.065$). Daily-level tests are exploratory given 16 effective events clustered within 21 winters.

At the episode level, non-SSW blocking suppresses natural activity in 82\% of episodes (median $\mathrm{RR} = 0.41$), whereas SSW-associated blocking suppresses only 42\% ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.95$; $P = 0.013$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:blocking_comparison}). This asymmetry reflects a scale distinction: the window-level suppression emerges from cumulative regime redistribution (cold-dry days double; warm-wet days halve; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:regimes}), not from stronger individual blocking episodes. SSW-blocking carries active moisture transport alongside cold advection, making individual episodes less purely suppressive.

Composite stratospheric diagnostics confirm the classic downward-propagation signature (Fig.~\ref{fig:epflux_composite}a). A height--time composite of zonal wind tendency ($du/dt$) across four pressure levels shows peak deceleration of $-6.6$~m~s$^{-1}$~d$^{-1}$ at 10~hPa ($t = 4.7$), propagating downward to $-1.1$~m~s$^{-1}$~d$^{-1}$ at 100~hPa ($t = 4.0$) in $\sim$6.6~days---quantitatively consistent with published EP-flux composites\cite{Polvani2004,Butler2017}. Direct composite $\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa---proportional to the vertical EP flux\cite{Polvani2004}---confirms systematically elevated wave forcing before every SSW event: the pre-onset anomaly averages $+9.4$~K$\cdot$m~s$^{-1}$ ($t = 7.7$, $P < 10^{-5}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:epflux_composite}b; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:vt_composite}). All 16 events show positive $\overline{v'T'}$ anomalies (range $18.7$--$44.7$~K$\cdot$m~s$^{-1}$), confirming that anomalous wave forcing is a universal precondition. The event-level dose--response is null ($\rho = -0.25$, $P = 0.34$; Fig.~\ref{fig:epflux_composite}c), but at $n = 16$ the test had only $\sim$50\% power to detect $|\rho| = 0.25$---the null is expected under range restriction (all SSW events require strong wave forcing by definition). SSW onset precedes peak Alpine blocking by $25 \pm 13$~days (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:blocking_lead}).

\subsection*{Daily regression models}

Daily count regression models decompose the association mechanistically but are labelled exploratory because 2,702 observation-days cluster within 21 winters containing only 16 SSW events. GEE with winter-level clustering yields $\mathrm{IRR} = 0.70$ ($P = 0.18$; 26\% power at the observed effect size; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:power_sim}). NB2 regression retains SSW significance ($\mathrm{IRR} = 0.55$, design-effect-corrected $P \approx 0.014$). The SSW--Z500 relative attribution is model-dependent (details in Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:nb_comparison}, \ref{tab:incremental}).

\subsection*{Weather--hazard amplification}

The composite weather shift is $d_{\mathrm{weather}} = 0.69$ but the hazard response is $d_{\mathrm{hazard}} = 1.06$---consistent with simultaneous weakening of multiple trigger channels. An illustrative threshold-amplification model shows that independent-channel solutions with $k = 6$--$8$ and correlated-channel solutions with $\sim$1--3 effective channels all reproduce the observed RR, whereas a single-channel model ($k = 1$) does not (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:threshold_amp}, \ref{tab:threshold_loo}, \ref{tab:corr_sensitivity}); however, the specific $k$ value is not uniquely identifiable at $n = 16$ (LOO range $k = 6$--$15$). The structural inference is that multi-channel triggering amplifies modest weather anomalies into a large hazard response. Multivariate directional coherence supports this: eight measurement channels (SWE, temperature, rainfall, snow fraction, natural dry-slab counts, danger ratings, human-triggered counts, faceted-crystal fraction) all show predicted-direction changes during SSW windows. Under independence the probability of 8/8 concordance is $P = 0.004$; adjusting for inter-channel correlation ($k_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 5$) yields $P = 0.031$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:combinatorial}). This directional coherence is reported as a consistency diagnostic, not as a standalone inferential test, because channel selection was guided by the loaded-gun hypothesis rather than pre-registered.

\subsection*{Robustness}

The suppression signal survives all robustness checks: leave-one-out (all 16 folds retain gmRR $< 1$), leave-two-out (all 120 pair combinations; range 0.35--0.55), specification curve (180 variants, 100\% directional consistency, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 10$), and Bayesian analysis ($\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$; Extended Data Tables~\ref{tab:loo}, \ref{tab:sensitivity}, \ref{tab:l2o}; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:spec_curve}). The BF and negative LOO BSS ($-0.95$) address different questions: the BF tests whether the central tendency shifts (strong evidence), while the BSS tests individual-event prediction (expected to fail for any binary rare-predictor model with high within-event variance, $\mathrm{CV} > 1.5$)---analogous to a treatment showing a consistent population-level effect without enabling individual prognosis. Three falsification tests confirm specificity: (1)~placebo dates $\pm$60~d show no effect; (2)~weak-vortex non-SSW winters yield gmRR $= 1.06$ ($P = 0.80$); (3)~sign-randomisation inference yields $P = 0.0005$ for the observed gmRR (Bonferroni-corrected $P_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.01$; Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:placebo}, \ref{tab:randomisation}).

The post-2005 subperiod ($n = 9$) preserves direction ($\mathrm{RR} = 0.45$) but not significance ($P = 0.18$; power $= 38\%$). An observation-artefact interpretation is directly falsified: during SSW windows, total avalanche reports \emph{increase} ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.15$), human-triggered incidents increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.45$), danger ratings increase in 11/14 events ($P = 0.02$), and wet natural avalanches increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.62$, $P = 0.038$)---only natural dry slabs decrease (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:observer}). If reduced observer effort caused the signal, \emph{all} types would decrease together. SSW event-type stratification shows type-invariant suppression: displacement ($n = 11$, gmRR $= 0.33$) and split-vortex events ($n = 5$, gmRR $= 0.32$) produce near-identical effects (Mann--Whitney $P = 0.95$), with the two contrary events spanning both morphologies (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:ssw_type}).

\subsection*{Snowpack mechanism: loading without triggering}

SNOWPACK diagnostics across 130 Swiss stations (292,837 station-days) characterise the loaded-gun state under ERA5-forced conditions. Full coverage is available for 11/16 events; the 5 incomplete events (predominantly early-period, including both contrary events) are not randomly missing, so SNOWPACK analyses are conditional on the 11-event subset. The primary inference does not depend on SNOWPACK. Because SNOWPACK shares ERA5 forcing with event identification, it quantifies process consistency rather than providing independent validation; the independent chain is provided by the French S2M/SAFRAN/Crocus system\cite{Vernay2022}, using separate reanalysis and distinct snow physics, and by the Swiss Avalanche Bulletin (human-expert assessment). Antecedent conditions ($-30$ to $-16$~d) are indistinguishable between SSW and non-SSW years ($P > 0.3$ for all metrics; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:pre_event_snowpack}).

Within that window, a four-step causal chain links SSW meteorological forcing to the loaded-gun snowpack state (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism_chain}; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:snowpack_mechanism}). Event-averaged estimates (each SSW event weighted equally) are reported as primary; station-day pooled estimates appear parenthetically because they better characterise process intensity but weight longer events more heavily. (1)~Air temperature drops by $3.0\,^\circ$C ($d = -0.58$), steepening the near-surface temperature gradient by 23\%---the primary driver of kinetic-growth metamorphism\cite{Schweizer2003}. The enhanced gradient penetrates to $\sim$1.0~m depth (thermal diffusivity $\kappa \approx 0.5 \times 10^{-6}$~m$^2$~s$^{-1}$; 1/$e$ penetration over 14-day SSW cold events $\approx 1.1$~m), placing the active faceting zone squarely within the human-triggerable depth band (0.2--1.2~m; Schweizer \& Jamieson\cite{Schweizer2008}; van Herwijnen \& Jamieson 2007). (2)~Persistent weak-layer prevalence increases by $+9.4\%$ event-averaged ($+25\%$ pooled, $d = 0.33$), peaking at $+4$~days after onset (Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:elev_strat}, \ref{tab:temporal_mechanism}). Penetration-depth analysis confirms that weak layers shift into human-triggerable positions: 43\% of SSW station-days have the most critical persistent weak layer within 0.2--1.2~m burial depth, versus 35\% in controls ($n = 281{,}522$; $d = 0.31$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:pwl_depth}). (3)~Structural stability (SSI) decreases by $10.2\%$ event-averaged ($24\%$ pooled, $d = -0.37$) and critical crack length by $20.3\%$ ($39\%$ pooled, $d = -0.35$). At the penetration depth itself, the critical crack length decreases by 33\% ($d = -0.32$), confirming that crack propagation at the weak-layer interface requires less energy during SSW windows. (4)~Natural triggers are suppressed: snow-surface warming $-57\%$, rain-on-snow $-29\%$, incoming shortwave $-17.5\%$, while the skier-triggering index decreases by 33\%---indicating greater human-trigger susceptibility. The event-averaged/pooled discrepancy (factor $\sim$2--3$\times$) reflects duration-weighting and is expected; both estimates are directionally consistent across all metrics. Wind transport increases by $+22.7\%$ (10/11 events; $d = 0.37$), driving wind-slab formation---but under cold, hard-slab conditions these instabilities shift from spontaneous to human-triggered release\cite{McClung2006}, consistent with the observed increase in human-triggered wind-slab incidents ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.45$) alongside natural dry-slab suppression. Precipitation-phase analysis confirms snow fraction increases by 12~pp while liquid precipitation decreases by 34\% ($P < 10^{-3}$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:precip_phase}).

This chain establishes a problem-type regime shift: persistent-slab conditions (PWL present, naturally stable yet skier-triggerable) increase by 29\% during SSW windows (47\% vs 36\% of station-days), while storm-slab conditions decrease. The ``loaded-gun'' signature---station-days that are simultaneously naturally stable (sn38~$> 2.0$) yet skier-triggerable (sk38~$< 1.5$) with a persistent weak layer present---is 1.24$\times$ more frequent during SSW windows, capturing the mechanistic paradox in a single diagnostic. The net trigger budget is $\Delta = +2.9\%$ (95\% CI $[-10.0, +16.8]$\%; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:trigger_budget})---statistically indistinguishable from zero---constituting direct evidence that the suppression is snowpack-structural rather than trigger-driven.

A four-regime weather decomposition shows that SSW windows nearly double cold-dry-day frequency while halving warm-wet days. Controlling for weather regime and day-of-year simultaneously attenuates the SSW effect to $\mathrm{IRR} = 0.89$ ($P = 0.06$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:regime_decomp})---consistent with partial overlap between SSW status and Alpine circulation regime. Detecting the residual effect at 80\% power would require $n \approx 380$ events, far exceeding the observational record; the operational value of SSW monitoring lies in the 2--4~week lead time it provides before the surface regime emerges, not in a residual direct effect. The linear Blinder--Oaxaca decomposition yields artefactual components exceeding $\pm 100\%$ (a known failure mode for nonlinear count data\cite{Bauer2008}); the multiplicative decomposition and IRR $= 0.89$ are the interpretable quantities.

Independent Rutschblock tests show a modest shift toward greater field-scale stability during SSW windows ($d = 0.10$, $P = 0.003$; Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:rutsch}). This does not contradict the loaded-gun mechanism but reflects a well-established scale distinction\cite{Schweizer2008,Reuter2018}: Rutschblock integrates near-surface slab hardness (which consolidates under cold-dry conditions), while SNOWPACK-derived deep metrics (PWL $+9.4\%$ event-averaged, SSI $-10.2\%$, CCL $-20.3\%$) capture buried weak-layer evolution. Penetration-depth analysis directly confirms this depth-dependent dissociation: station-days with critically unstable weak layers at human-triggerable depths (sk38~$< 1.5$ with PWL in 0.2--1.2~m) increase by 35\% during SSW windows ($n = 281{,}522$). Cold consolidation improves near-surface slab resistance without removing the persistent weakness below; this is the physical basis of the loaded-gun state.

\section*{Discussion}

We report the first demonstration that stratospheric variability measurably modulates a threshold-governed natural hazard: Swiss natural dry slab avalanche counts fall by two-thirds during SSW windows ($\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$; event-level sign-randomisation $P_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.01$) while human-triggered fatalities increase ($\mathrm{RR} = 1.40$; CMH $P = 0.007$). Five measurement systems converge on consistent directional results ($P = 0.031$ under independence). Three independent lines of evidence confirm the loaded-gun mechanism: (i)~SNOWPACK process diagnostics show persistent weak layers shifting into the human-triggerable depth band ($d = 0.31$; critical crack length $-$33\%; 281,522 station-days); (ii)~Swiss Avalanche Bulletin danger ratings increase during SSW windows (47\% vs 37\% rated `considerable' or higher; OR~$= 1.49$), providing human-expert confirmation independent of the model chain; and (iii)~the 2021 SSW directionally confirmed the prediction out-of-sample ($P = 0.001$). The atmospheric precursor mechanism is established at $n \approx 40$ across the published SSW literature\cite{Butler2017,Kidston2015,Baldwin2021}; the hazard-translation step reported here extends this chain to a threshold-governed geophysical system for the first time.

Z500 carries the surface signal ($\rho = 0.64$, $P = 0.008$), and the regime-conditioned residual SSW effect is attenuated ($\mathrm{IRR} = 0.89$, $P = 0.06$)---consistent with partial overlap between SSW status and Alpine circulation regime, as expected when conditioning on a variable that lies on the causal pathway. SSW events are the upstream precursor that \emph{organises} surface weather into the loaded-gun configuration, providing a recognisable regime signature\cite{Sigmond2013} with 2--4~weeks' lead time beyond surface-regime forecasts. The atmospheric mechanism---SSW $\to$ enhanced blocking $\to$ European cold anomaly---is independently established across ${\sim}40$ events in four reanalyses\cite{Butler2017,Baldwin2021,Kidston2015,Charlton2007} (Butler et al.\ $n = 37$; Kidston et al.\ $n = 27$; Kretschmer et al.\ $n = 26$; Baldwin \& Dunkerton $n = 18$); our 16-event subsample is representative of this larger population (KS test $P = 0.57$). The novel contribution requiring $n = 16$ is the final translation step: cold-dry regime $\to$ snowpack instability $\to$ avalanche suppression. The loaded-gun mechanism is specific to the wave-forced sub-class of blocking---where active planetary wave forcing sustains moisture transport alongside cold advection\cite{Polvani2004}---rather than to blocking \emph{per se}; SSW-associated blocking suppresses natural avalanches at 2.3$\times$ the rate of non-SSW blocking episodes ($P = 0.013$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:blocking_comparison}).

\paragraph{Compound-event extension and global scope.} SSW-driven cold-air outbreaks affect continuous-response systems globally\cite{Cohen2021,Gasparrini2015}: the February 2021 Texas crisis (over 200 deaths, \$100~billion damages) demonstrated catastrophic societal consequences. Avalanches provide the first threshold-governed test case, and the loaded-gun mechanism we identify---where a single forcing drives structural loading and trigger suppression in opposing directions---extends the Zscheischler et al.\cite{Zscheischler2020} preconditioned-event category into a formally distinct sub-type applicable beyond avalanches. The four defining conditions (preconditioned substrate, simultaneous loading and trigger suppression, opposing-direction response, delayed human-trigger realisation) are all satisfied here. Three independent lines of evidence converge: (i)~SNOWPACK process diagnostics across 281,522 station-days show persistent weak layers shifting into the human-triggerable depth band ($d = 0.31$, CCL $-$33\%), with persistent-slab conditions increasing 29\%; (ii)~Swiss Avalanche Bulletin danger ratings---a human-expert assessment independent of the model chain---confirm elevated hazard (OR~$= 1.49$; $P < 10^{-10}$) simultaneous with natural-release suppression; and (iii)~the January 2021 SSW---post-dating all calibration data---directionally confirmed the prediction (15/17, $P = 0.001$; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:prospective_2021}). The loaded-gun framework is not Alpine-specific: any threshold-governed hazard system where SSW-forced weather-regime shifts differentially affect loading and triggering may exhibit opposing-direction compound behaviour. Published SSW surface impacts extend to North American cold extremes\cite{Kolstad2010} (cold-extreme probability $+3\times$ in eastern North America), East Asian cold surges, and Scandinavian hazard conditions (our Norwegian result: $P = 0.015$). Candidate loaded-gun configurations include cold-air damming during atmospheric rivers, permafrost degradation masked by refreezing, drought-to-flood transitions, and river-ice breakup. SSW events occur in $\sim$60\% of winters\cite{Charlton2007}, projected stable through 2100\cite{Ayarzaguena2020}; the risk-communication paradox (fewer precursors when structural hazard peaks) applies wherever threshold-governed hazards respond to weather-regime shifts.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The $n = 16$ event sample constrains all hazard-translation analyses (GEE power 26\%; 72 events needed for 80\% power); the atmospheric precursor mechanism is validated at $n \approx 40$ in the published SSW literature. Only 3/12 event-level tests survive BH-FDR; inference rests on the sign test ($P = 0.002$), supported by $\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$, sign-randomisation $P_{\mathrm{adj}} = 0.01$, and event-level permutation ($P = 0.020$). The Bayesian posterior for the suppression rate ($P(\theta > 0.5) = 0.975$; 95\% HDI $[0.50, 0.90]$) confirms robust evidence for a population-level effect under weak priors. The wave-forcing dose--response is null ($P = 0.34$) but underpowered ($\sim$50\% at $n = 16$). Daily regression corrected for overdispersion ($\hat{\varphi} = 45.1$) gives $\Delta \mathrm{QAIC} = -0.6$ and quasi-Poisson $P = 0.065$---marginal incremental value of the SSW predictor beyond Z500 at the daily level; the NB2 model ($\Delta \mathrm{AIC} = -14.2$) provides moderate support. LOO BSS ($= -0.95$) tests individual-event prediction, which is expected to fail for any binary rare-predictor model with high within-event variance ($\mathrm{CV} > 1.5$); the BF tests population-level shift (strong evidence). SNOWPACK diagnostics share ERA5 forcing; independent validation is provided by the French S2M chain\cite{Vernay2022} and Swiss Avalanche Bulletin danger ratings (OR~$= 1.49$; $P < 10^{-10}$). All multi-country data share the same 16 SSW events. Single-author pipeline; open code and data enable independent replication.

Candidate loaded-gun configurations in other threshold-governed systems---cold-air damming during atmospheric rivers, permafrost degradation masked by refreezing, drought-to-flood transitions, river-ice breakup---remain untested. SSW events occur in $\sim$60\% of winters with surface impacts documented across North America\cite{Kolstad2010}, East Asia, and Scandinavia; any hazard system where loading and triggering respond differentially to SSW-forced regime shifts may exhibit opposing-direction compound behaviour. This study provides a template for their identification and a quantitative framework for assessing whether operational SSW monitoring can improve hazard lead times.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Datasets}

The primary outcome was the Swiss WSL/SLF daily count of natural-trigger dry slab avalanches ($\geq$size~1) across 21 winters (1998/99--2018/19; 7,518~days). An independent outcome---the SLF fatal and near-fatal incident database (1970--2025; 4,868 records)---was used for extended historical ($n = 29$) and prospective (January~2021) analyses. Supporting datasets: Utah Avalanche Center dry slab record (2012--2025), Norwegian NVE danger forecasts (10 inland regions, 2017/18--2022/23), reconstructed M\'et\'eo-France BRA bulletins (14 northern-Alpine massifs, 2016/17--2022/23), Avalanche Canada flexible-forecast panel (post-2022), EAWS danger levels (2011--2015), Swiss EnviDat danger levels and human-triggered counts, ALBINA, Austrian LAWIS incidents (1992--2024), Swiss Rutschblock tests, and SNOWPACK simulations from 130 Swiss stations. Details are in the SI.

\subsection*{SSW identification and matched-control design}

SSW events were identified from the Butler et al.\ catalog\cite{Butler2015} as reversals of the 10~hPa 60$^\circ$N zonal-mean zonal wind. For each event, we computed $\mathrm{RR} = O / E$, where $O$ is the observed count in a $\pm$15-day window and $E$ is the day-of-year-matched expectation from non-SSW winters only ($\pm$3 DOY bandwidth). This design controls seasonality and prevents SSW contamination of the reference climatology. For ordinal danger data we compared SSW and matched-control distributions.

\subsection*{Diagnostics}

ERA5 daily fields for the Swiss Alps (temperature, precipitation, snowfall, wind, radiation) diagnosed surface anomalies. SNOWPACK model output (130 stations, 2004--2013; 11/16 SSW events) provided loading, stability and persistent-weak-layer diagnostics; trigger-frequency metrics were derived from station-level warming, rain-on-snow and radiative conditions. The SNOWPACK penetration-depth diagnostic (Pen\_depth) was used to characterise burial depth of the most critical persistent weak layer at each station-day; critical crack length at that depth (min\_ccl\_pen) quantified propagation susceptibility. Swiss Avalanche Bulletin danger levels (EnviDat; $n = 285{,}157$ station-days) provided an independent human-expert validation of the loaded-gun mechanism, as the bulletin assessment is not derived from the SNOWPACK model chain. NCEP reanalysis (SLP, Z500, U850) quantified large-scale circulation anomalies across the full 1998--2019 period. Stratospheric wave forcing was diagnosed using two standard scalar EP-flux proxy diagnostics from NCEP--NCAR reanalysis (independent of ERA5): (1)~zonal-mean poleward eddy heat flux $\overline{v'T'}$ at 100~hPa (45--75$^\circ$N, cos-latitude-weighted), proportional to the vertical component of the Eliassen--Palm flux $F_z$ and widely used as the benchmark measure of upward wave-activity flux\cite{Polvani2004}; and (2)~zonal-mean zonal wind deceleration $\partial \bar{u}/\partial t$ at 10~hPa, a proxy for EP-flux convergence $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}$ integrated over the polar cap. These diagnostics were evaluated at six pressure levels (10, 20, 30, 50, 70, 100~hPa) to characterise the vertical propagation structure. Our 16-event composites are quantitatively consistent with the published multi-reanalysis composites for ${\sim}40$ SSW events (Butler et al.\cite{Butler2017}: peak $du/dt \approx -5.8$~m~s$^{-1}$~d$^{-1}$ at 10~hPa; our composite: $-6.6$~m~s$^{-1}$~d$^{-1}$). Two-sample KS testing confirms our subsample is representative of the full SSW population ($P = 0.57$). Full latitude--pressure EP-flux cross-sections ($F_z(y,p)$, $F_y(y,p)$) were not computed; the scalar proxies capture the vertically-resolved timing and magnitude of wave driving relevant to stratosphere--troposphere coupling but do not resolve meridional structure. The French S2M reanalysis\cite{Vernay2022} (SAFRAN/Crocus/MEPRA chain, independent of ERA5/SNOWPACK) provided cross-model validation; SAFRAN has been validated against 665 independent snow-depth measurements\cite{Durand2009}.

\subsection*{Statistics}

Primary inference used 16 event-level rate ratios assessed with sign test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, $t$-test and permutation test (10,000 random-date sets). Bootstrap CIs from 10,000 resamples; winter-blocked bootstrap confirms robustness (Supplementary Table~34c). BH-FDR correction stratified into event-level, daily-level and external families. The six adequately powered tests---sign test, bootstrap CI, BF, 180-specification curve, Z500 partial $\rho$, and sign-randomisation---constitute the primary evidence; mechanistic decomposition tests (mediation, dose--response, GEE) are explicitly labelled exploratory at $n = 16$. Power analysis: exact sign test at $n = 16$ achieves 79\% power at the observed effect; the 180-specification curve achieves 100\% directional consistency; Bayesian $\mathrm{BF} = 17.9$--$178.7$. These benchmarks match the foundational SSW-surface literature ($\sim$18 events in Baldwin \& Dunkerton\cite{Baldwin2001}; 15--25 standard per Domeisen et al.\cite{Domeisen2020}).
